# Coupled corners and finished paper

## Construction

The first version 2 pattern is an open Masu box made from a single square. The square spans four units along each diagonal. A half-unit grid with bisected cells preserves the material lines of the blintzes, the wall creases and the diagonal pleats, including their reflected plies. Its 128 triangles share 81 material vertices. The finished floor is one unit square and the walls are half a unit high.

The first twelve actions find the center, blintz the corners, mark the floor boundaries, reopen the end flaps and stand the side walls upright. Each subsequent end takes three actions. Raise the wall while folding both gussets inward, bend the terminal triangle outward at the future floor crease, then roll the end over the rim and seat the point on the floor. Separating the last two bends keeps the tip above the base throughout the wrap. The end linings cover the gussets and the four original corners meet on the inside floor. This is an original coordinate construction and manuscript for the traditional pattern. Reference sequences and their differing difficulty ratings are in the [feasibility matrix](../phase-8-models.md).

Ordinary rotations and the flat packet author remain available. A right gusset adds two rigid triangles between an upright side and a turning end wall. In local coordinates the side ray is vertical and the end ray starts horizontal. At wall angle θ, the common unit-cell diagonal has vertical component 1 and horizontal components of magnitudes k and √(1 − k²), where k = cos θ / (1 + sin θ). Its length stays √2 and its projections onto both boundary rays remain 1. This determines both triangle orientations through closure without interpolating vertex positions. One triangle turns around the upright post. The other turns around the end flap's edge and follows the end wall. The same operation works at all four reflected corners. Near the open configuration the gusset angle changes faster than the wall angle. Timing follows the authored wall rotation. Elasticity and resistance from real paper thickness remain outside the calculation. A version 2 author names those two packets, their signed directions and the carrying wall crease.

Compilation checks the adjacent packet traces before choosing the owner of a shared vertex. A fixed neighbor owns the stationary seam. Playback samples the canonical starting pose and the requested fraction in either direction. The drawing applies the same rotations to small ply offsets. A wall wrap can specify a three-dimensional virtual hinge origin in ply units. The user can orbit underneath the box. Multiple active guides identify the wall and the two corner diagonals. This mechanism covers the orthogonal box corner. A waterbomb, general reverse fold or petal eversion needs its own construction and independent checks.

## Catalog and appearance

Each published entry owns a permanent route ID, paper type, category, maker difficulty, action count and three sRGB finish colors. Explore groups only populated tiers and counts the entries in each group. IDs and transcripts survive reordering. The shared studio uses the existing sage/gold materials until the sampled position equals the action count. It changes the front, reverse and edge pigments at that endpoint. The change runs on the same drawing frame as the mesh, including reduced-motion jumps. Lighting, edge depth, cast shadows and vertex positions follow the existing renderer.

The production build writes a transparent 440 × 312 PNG per model. The projector uses the last canonical pose, winding, drawn edges and per-face ply separation used in the studio. A depth buffer resolves overlapping layers. An orthographic camera, diffuse face shading and twice-per-axis sampling keep the small image legible. The homepage references a cacheable static image URL. It loads neither the projector nor Three.js. The model name and description beside each decorative preview provide its accessible text. The dev middleware serves the same image construction.

## Acceptance

For a new model, compare material geometry with independent coordinates, verify which original flaps are visible on each surface, exercise the full timeline and compare rendered and gallery silhouettes against independently constructed outlines. Record actual measurements and any untested real-paper properties. [Verification and scope](../release.md)

For a hands-on Masu trial, use a 150 mm square. The ideal floor is about 53.0 mm on each side and the rim about 26.5 mm high. Record which panels lie inside each wall, where the four tips meet, and whether the box stays open after release. Record the paper weight and any adjustment needed to accommodate its thickness. This trial remains open.
