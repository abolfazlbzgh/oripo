# Three advanced constructions

## Selection before authoring

These are original Oripo constructions developed for this project. The sheet layouts, ratios, fold sequences, wording and projected artwork are independently authored. No external designer's diagram, crease file, photo, model mesh or translated instructions are used. The external references describe maker skills, general kinematics and rights. They supply no permission to adapt a particular pictured design. This record proposes project ratings. Independent folders have not rated these exact designs. ### Pinwheel calyx · `pinwheel-calyx`

Select a shallow four-lobed flower with clockwise pinched ribs, four turned lips and a continuous diamond-shaped receptacle. Use one thin, two-sided 24 cm square. On the normalized square from −1 to 1, the northeast petal attaches along the chord from the right midpoint B to the top midpoint E. Rotate this layout into the other three corners.

1. Valley-fold the four corner tips inward on the short chords joining points 0.22 units from each corner. The small doubled tips sit on the inner surface.
2. Raise the northeast and southwest flaps about the midpoint chords. Their roots remain attached along the full chord.
3. Raise the other opposite pair to form the four walls of the calyx. Use a working opening of 72° from the sheet.
4. Pinch the first opposite pair. Looking at the original northeast corner, its rib runs from B toward a point 0.28 units left of the top-right tip. Along the rib, set two reverse stations 0.60 and 0.77 units from B. Each chevron retreats toward its free edge with longitudinal run 1.25 per unit of cross-rib travel. Hold the broad side against the receptacle, valley the rib, and reverse its middle segment into a mountain while simultaneously returning the doubled lip. The held side of the lower rib stays seated.
5. Form the matching inside/outside crimp in the second pair. The four pockets must retain an opening, the two plies must travel together around the upper reverse, and the tips must turn the same way in plan.

The maker must register two oblique reverses through a hem on an already erected petal, then repeat with consistent handedness. OrigamiUSA places crimps at High Intermediate. It rates a conventional tulip substantially lower. We claim neither a lotus sink nor an eight-petal eversion. Preserve hemming and raising as ordinary current-axis turns. A new local, planar-packet crimp must work in the erected plane. Require fixed root and throat widths, independently constructed rim/spine positions, positive inter-petal gaps, distinct hem layers and underside projections. Reject an attributed eight-petal flower without permission and a demonstrated eversion. If a pair collides, first test the turning direction and aperture on a single attached flap.

### River tern · `river-tern`

Select a full bird with a long tail, two independently spread, pointed wings, an elevated neck and a lower crown that carries the doubled bill. Use one thin 24 cm square held as a diamond, with corners at unit distance from its center.

1. Fold the forward tip down at y = 0.77. It lands at y = 0.54. The doubled forepart participates in the neck crimp.
2. Book-fold the right half to the left along the tail-to-bill spine, keeping the hem inside. With the two sides registered, form and release an oblique neck mark through the packet.
3. Form and release a second parallel return mark. In opened-sheet coordinates the marks cross the spine at y = 0.55 and 0.67 and run toward the head by 0.72 units per unit of lateral travel.
4. Open the book. Gather both sides of the spine while reversing the two marked stations in opposite senses. The throat turns upward and the outer bill points forward again. The distal part reverses inside the folded cheeks. Keep the wide wing panels free.
5. Spread the left wing on its long hinge at x = −0.30, then the right at x = 0.30. These turns use the spatial wing roots. The tail and the raised bridge of the neck remain in place.

A standard crane is Intermediate in OrigamiUSA's sequential scale. The doubled, accurately registered inside/outside crimp and the separate wing packets are the basis of this High Intermediate selection. Reuse the calyx's crimp with a symmetric, free book around a stationary spine. Check both wings independently, the two longitudinally parallel bill/tail directions, the height of the crimp, bill thickness and transverse collisions. If the outer wing hinge crosses the neck, move its root out to a straight unbroken chord before publication. A generic single-reverse crane is rejected as a fallback.

### Amber hex iris · `hex-iris`

Select a hexagonal relief with a closed six-sided center, three different pairs of sloping vanes and six connecting gussets. Use one regular thin-paper hexagon, 24 cm point to point. The sheet is supplied in that shape. The central hexagon is concentric and similarly oriented. Use an inner circumradius of 0.60 in a unit-circumradius sheet.

1. Each pair of opposite inner-hexagon sides forms two straight valley references. Fold the opposite outer margins inward and reopen. Repeat with the other two pairs. Their six intersections bound the hub.
2. Locate two outward rails from the ends of each hub side. They are parallel and tilted counterclockwise from its outward normal by arctan(1/√15), about 14.48°. Pinch the valley/mountain boundaries of the gussets as the six side bands come up. Keep the central panel flat. Three different opening angles recur twice around the ring. The quicker vanes lead the slower pair without exchanging their material order.
3. Close all six bands onto the hub with the gussets between their neighboring vanes. Then ease the same creases open to a relief angle. The fixed central polygon, exposed vanes and underside must remain distinguishable. The final form deliberately exhibits the space inside the folds.

OrigamiUSA places a closed hexagonal twist at High Intermediate and a standard square twist and Miura corrugation lower. Getting the sixth corner to close with the first is the maker's precise, coordinated task. A polygon of rigidly folding four-valent vertices needs consistent fold-angle multipliers around the loop. Our angle, boundary, branch order and teaching sequence are independently specified. [General polygon closure criterion, Evans et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4593671/)

Use one new rigid polygon field. For adjacent vane half-angle tangents u and v, rail closure gives A·v² − 2uv + B·u² = 0, with A = cos(60°) + tan(skew) sin(60°) and B = 1 − A. Let h = (3+√5)/2. The two possible ratios are h and 1/h². The ordered factors h, h, 1/h² repeat twice and multiply to one. Each gusset must preserve its included 60° angle and both rail lengths. Prove central stationarity, independent boundary intersections at the flat closure, positive heights on reopening, sided contact order and no transverse penetration. If the specified ring intersects, investigate a narrower exterior band or an independently laid out rhombus twist. An endpoint-only twist and another Miura grid are rejected.

All three decisions are selections for implementation. Public availability depends on their individual moving-paper, collision, silhouette and playback results.
