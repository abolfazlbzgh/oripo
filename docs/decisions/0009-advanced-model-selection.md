# Three original Advanced constructions

## Selection and provenance

Before implementation, commit `4871e6c` selected a crimped flower, a full bird and a closed hexagonal twist. Pose fixtures led to two revisions. The shallow first flower resembled a tray, so the selected construction gained a blintz base and a second whorl of petals. The bird gained tapered cheeks, a tail reverse and swept wings so the bill, fork and wrists read separately in profile. The plans below describe the published geometry.

These are original Oripo designs. The project authored the crease layouts, ratios, diagrams, code, instructions and translations. The previews project the project's own mesh. No external design or diagram was adapted. OrigamiUSA's criteria rate physical techniques. The project applied those criteria to these particular sequences. Independent ratings and hands-on trials of the three originals remain open. Online model galleries served as visual complexity references only. ## Pinwheel calyx · `pinwheel-calyx`

One thin, two-sided 34 cm square forms four curved outer lips and four pointed inner petals standing around an open diamond. The coordinates below put the original corners at (±2, 0) and (0, ±2). Distances are in model units unless given in centimeters.

1. **Blintz the base.** Valley-fold all four points to the center. The new square has corners (±1, ±1). Four loose original points lie on top.
2. **Hem the four corners.** Valley-fold both layers on each short chord 0.22 units in from its corner. The doubled rim has four plies where the hem lands.
3. **Raise the first pair.** Valley-fold the northeast and southwest corner packets on chords between adjacent side midpoints. Stop at 49° and leave the central diamond fixed.
4. **Raise the second pair.** Lift the other opposite packets to 49°. Look for an open basin and four independently accessible corner packets.
5. **Crimp two raised petals.** In the northeast flap's own plane, follow the rib from (1, 0) toward (0.72, 1). At rib distances 0.60 and 0.77, mark symmetric chevrons with longitudinal run 1.25 per transverse unit. Hold the broad side against the base. Pinch the rib as a valley, reverse the middle section into a mountain, then return the distal segment to its original bearing. Carry the four-ply hem through the return. The rib half-angle is 52°. Repeat on the opposite flap.
6. **Crimp the other two.** Rotate the layout through a quarter turn and repeat with the same handedness. Each outer lip turns clockwise while the receptacle stays flat.
7. **Lift the two side petals.** Select only the loose tips of the east and west blintz flaps. Valley-fold each to 78° on its short base 0.46 units from the center.
8. **Lift the other inner petals.** Raise the north and south tips to 65°. The two heights distinguish the inner whorl from the four curled lips.

OrigamiUSA places crimps at High Intermediate. Here the maker registers two obliques in an erected, hemmed packet, reverses the rib twice and frees four single inner flaps. A standard tulip or lily does not establish that rating. The earlier attributed eight-petal sink design remains withheld. Its layered eversion and rights would require separate evidence. The original shallow flower served as the rejected geometric prototype. [OrigamiUSA difficulty](https://www.origamiusa.org/difficulty)

Ordinary turns lift and hem each packet. The spatial crimp uses the raised plane and material pins to hold the broad side still. Independently constructed bearings locate the folded rim at (0.580132, 0.544750, 0.245521). Opposite rim points are 1.591610 apart. The two inner-tip heights are 0.46 sin(78°) and 0.46 sin(65°). A material probe checks the four-ply order inside a hem.

## River tern · `river-tern`

One thin, two-sided 24 cm square makes a long neck, pointed bill, forked tail, two swept wings and two raised wrists. Lay the sheet on a point with its forward corner at (0, 1.5) and the tail at (0, −1.5). The other two corners are (±1.5, 0).

1. **Form the kite.** Valley-fold both forward edges onto the vertical diagonal, leaving two long gate flaps over the front half.
2. **Double the bill.** Valley-fold the forward tip through the packet at y = 1.35.
3. **Taper the cheeks.** Mountain-fold the cap's two corners under on obliques of slope magnitude 0.80. Their upper ends meet at the bill.
4. **Close the book.** Valley-fold the right packet onto the left along the entire spine with the bill inside.
5. **Mark the throat.** Valley-fold the front packet across the line meeting the spine at y = 0.40, with longitudinal run 1.30 per lateral unit.
6. **Release the throat.** Reopen that mark to the closed book. Both cheeks retain matching lower obliques.
7. **Mark the crown.** Mountain-fold the same stacked front on a parallel line at y = 0.76.
8. **Release the crown.** Undo the mountain to recover the book and its second set of marks.
9. **Mark the tail.** Valley-fold the back packet at y = −1.20 on a shallower oblique with run 0.36.
10. **Release the tail.** Reopen the back packet while keeping its two edges aligned.
11. **Open the book.** Return the right half to its side. Preserve the kite, cheeks and doubled bill.
12. **Reverse the neck and tail.** Raise both sides around the keel to a 63° half-opening. Push the throat inside, turn the crown back outside and fold the rear point between the tail edges. The throat and crown ride on the same oblique pair. The tail uses its own opposite-facing frame on the shared keel.
13. **Spread the left wing.** Hold the keel and open the left wing through 70° about its current swept root, |x| = 0.39 + 0.11y.
14. **Spread the right wing.** Open its matching root through 70°. The bill and tail stay in place.
15. **Raise the wrists.** Turn both tips up through 48° on |x| = 0.70 − 0.20y. The broad wing panels keep their bearings.

The maker has to register marks through a book, reverse the multilayer neck twice while setting a forked tail and isolate two wing packets. This supports High Intermediate on OrigamiUSA's sequential scale. A standard crane, commonly Intermediate, was rejected for this slot. The prototype's longer unshaped front and straighter wing line were replaced before publication. [OrigamiUSA difficulty](https://www.origamiusa.org/difficulty)

The crimp is the same primitive as in the flower, with the center spine held. The symmetric throat/crown reflection returns the distal bill to a parallel bearing. The intervening neck rises 0.335756 units. The right lifted wrist ends at (0.685872, −0.590234, 0.264762). A sample within the bill has four plies after hemming, eight after the cheek tuck and sixteen in the closed book. Reopening returns the eight alternating plies to the original order, which follows the distal neck normal through the reverse. The geometry fixture predicts the position of every material vertex during the neck reverse and both wing actions using independent spatial rotations.

## Amber hex iris · `hex-iris`

Supply one thin, two-sided regular hexagon, 24 cm between opposite points. A hexagon supplied in this shape is the starting material. Put one vertex to the right. The six center marks lie at 0.60 of each radius, or 7.2 cm. They bound a stationary, even-sided hub. The outer vanes are joined by six triangular gussets.

Each row below specifies two actions. Fold opposite margins on the full marked lines, one toward you and one away, then undo both completely. Full reference lines are creased during preparation. Only the finite hub sides and outward rail segments move in the final twist.

| Actions | References and packet                                                                |
| ------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1–2     | Mark and release the upper-right and lower-left sides of the hub.                    |
| 3–4     | Mark and release the top and bottom sides of the hub.                                |
| 5–6     | Mark and release the upper-left and lower-right sides of the hub.                    |
| 7–8     | Mark and release the farther parallel rails of the upper-right and lower-left vanes. |
| 9–10    | Mark and release the corresponding far rails at top and bottom.                      |
| 11–12   | Mark and release the far rails at upper left and lower right.                        |
| 13–14   | Mark and release the nearer parallel rails at upper right and lower left.            |
| 15–16   | Mark and release the near rails at top and bottom.                                   |
| 17–18   | Mark and release the near rails at upper left and lower right.                       |

At each side, the two rails run from the hub corners at a counterclockwise tilt of arctan(1/√15), about 14.48°, from the outward perpendicular. The far and near parallels are about 5.0 and 1.9 cm from the sheet center.

19. **Close the iris.** Turn all six hub sides as valleys. Lead with the upper-left and lower-right vanes, placing this pair closest to the hub on closure. Both their rails are mountains. Top and bottom follow with the far rail a mountain and the near rail a valley. The remaining vanes have two valley rails. Let each gusset fold between its neighbors until the connected ring is flat.
20. **Open the relief.** Ease the same six vanes back together along their creases. Stop at fraction 0.26 of the closing path. Three opposing pairs reach different elevations, exposing the relief from both sides.

OrigamiUSA lists closed hexagonal twists at High Intermediate. Maintaining six consistent rail bearings, bringing all vanes in together and preserving the accumulated layer order makes this more demanding than the low-intermediate herringbone. Another corrugation was rejected. Narrowing the vane band or investigating a rhombus twist were fallbacks if the ring could not close. [OrigamiUSA difficulty](https://www.origamiusa.org/difficulty)

For neighboring vane half-angle tangents u and v, the 60° gusset requires A v² − 2uv + B u² = 0, with A = cos(60°) + tan(skew) sin(60°) and B = 1 − A. Set h = (3 + √5)/2. The neighboring ratios h, h, 1/h² repeat twice. Their product closes the last rail against the first. The parameter, sheet and teaching sequence are original. The general polygon-closure criterion is a mathematical reference. [Evans et al., polygon closure](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4593671/)

The closure fixture checks all six included angles and terminal rail lengths. An independent sector construction predicts the vane and gusset corners. An incoming-height probe checks 2,681 touching ply pairs against their drawn order. The reopened relief is 0.383371 units high. [Measured results and limitations](../release.md)
