## Butterfly · B1

The construction under evaluation is WWF Together’s traditional Monarch Butterfly from one square. Its double-boat base, two hindwing folds, corner tucks, central book and pinched wing roots correspond to the Intermediate butterfly on Kids Web Japan. WWF identifies the underlying design as traditional. Its illustrations and prose are copyrighted. Use original coordinates, manuscript and projections. The public sources remain external. The basic comparison tutorial supplies no difficulty claim for B1. Independent maker and owner visual review are pending. [WWF sequence, pp. 3–4](https://files.worldwildlife.org/wwfcmsprod/files/Publication/file/1z79g6ntm4_WWF_Together_ButterflyOrigami.pdf) · [Web Japan base](https://web-japan.org/kidsweb/virtual/origami/exploring02m.html) · [Web Japan finish](https://web-japan.org/kidsweb/virtual/origami/exploring02n.html) · [Abrashi’s traditional windmill butterfly](https://abrashiorigami.com/butterfly/) · [Web Japan reuse terms](https://web-japan.org/plaza/about.html)

**Construction lock.** Use a square spanning −1 to +1 in both material axes. Mark and reopen the diagonals. Blintz all four points, mark the second square on the reverse and release the two sets of creases. Gate both sides to the center along x = ±0.5. Pull out the two loose corners at one end while lowering its back wall, then repeat at the other end. Each original corner becomes a boat point. Book the two boats together at y = 0. Swing only the two free points on the front boat across the diagonals from the center to (±0.5, −0.5). Taper the two outer hindwing corners. Pinch the center book and spread the left and right wing pairs on their oblique roots. A mirrored packet needs its own hinge and the front boat must clear the rear.

**Independent targets.** In the plan view before pinching, the two outer forewing tips are at (±1, 0), the broad shoulders at (±0.5, −0.5) and both free hindwing tips reach (0, −1). In the final elevated frontal view, expect two broad forewings, two narrower hindwings with separate tips, a narrow central pleat and bilateral relief. Compare the full-size and card-size outline with WWF step 17. Use violet or blue stock. Verify layer order through the boat collapse, the accessibility of each loose flap and the rear view. The numerical body pinch and small wing-taper folds require checking against the drawn landmarks. The owner approved the visual direction only.

**Local publication.** The 18 actions keep all four original corners as wing tips. The two top tips span the widest edge and the two tapered lower tips enclose an open notch. The signed 75° spread gives the mirrored wings relief along a narrow raised spine. The source drawing is a slightly asymmetric oblique view. The studio presents a more frontal violet butterfly and shows the diagonal precreases. Independent measurements use the plan-view polygons folded about the sloping roots. The source gives the topology and visible landmarks. The numeric taper, spread and drawing thickness are Oripo choices. The finished front, reverse and edge use `#8772c1`, `#a38ed1` and `#453463`. [Package](../models/monarch-butterfly/lesson.json) · [Independent outline](../tests/butterfly-outline.ts)

The map passed the complete nine-model migration before replacement. Its material package remains a test fixture. Each old localized map URL returns its locale’s noindex HTTP 404 with recovery links. Each butterfly URL has its own canonical, translations and initial progress. Historical map manuscripts are available in the migration commit.

## Layered lotus · L1

The reference for the harder candidate is Joost Langeveld’s single-square _Origami Lotus Flower_, steps 1–30. Starting from the blintzed square, its four flaps are squashed, tucked and opened to free the petal layers. The base is opened and sunk. Four thinner petals turn inward and four thicker outer petals roll outward. A rose-colored version should show eight tips in two rings around an open center from overhead, and two distinct heights in an oblique view. The outer and inner curls are explicit in steps 29–30. The tutorial does not establish an Advanced maker rating or a redistribution grant for an adaptation. No Langeveld illustrations are included. [Designer’s complete fold sequence](https://www.joostlangeveldorigami.nl/diagramslotus.html)

The two traditional lotus leads describe the successive blintzes and drawing the underside around the folded rim. Their publishers rate these versions Easy. A second traditional single-square eight-petal flower is rated Intermediate and described as a stemmed flower. None establishes an Advanced layered lotus for this slot. The engine can fold the blintzes. L1 still needs a constrained central sink and a verified material path for curling the petals with clearance between layers. Next, secure an adaptable Advanced construction and establish one petal and its surrounding material on paper before extending the solver. **Pinwheel calyx remains published.** [Traditional lotus and rating](https://origami.me/lotus/) · [Origami Guide lotus](https://origami.guide/origami-flowers/origami-lotus-flower/) · [Traditional eight-petal comparison](https://origami.guide/?p=10186)

## Six-point twist star · S1

Shuzo Fujimoto’s _Hex Twist Star, CFW 106_ places the CFW 91 central molecule on a 6 × 6 × 6 triangular grid in a regular hexagon. From above, six equally spaced rays should read around a structured central star. An oblique view should show the layering of the twist. The intended stock is teal or gold. Fujimoto’s heirs released the five self-published books into the public domain. Kosmulski’s modern photos have separate credit and licensing. The project uses links only. [CFW 106 model and grid](https://origami.kosmulski.org/models/hex-twist-star-cfw-106) · [Heirs’ public-domain release](https://origami.kosmulski.org/blog/2022-10-23-fujimoto-books-public-domain)

The CFW 106 reference identifies the starting twist and finished views in _Twist Origami I_, p. 10, and _Invitation to Creative Playing with Origami_, p. 103. The CFW 91 references likewise identify a starting twist and finished photos. The CFW 143 alternative adds hints. These records leave the ordered extraction, face ownership and over/under path of the six outer rays unresolved. OrigamiUSA also lists a traditional six-pointed star, without identifying the Fujimoto twist or an Advanced maker rating. The existing iris field fixes the rail sine at ¼ and uses its own six-vane closure. Substituting its hub radius cannot supply the missing star packets. Next, verify a scored central molecule, its layer order and one outer sector against a physical fold, then validate rotational closure of all six sectors. **Amber hex iris remains published.** [CFW 106 instruction record](https://origami.kosmulski.org/instructions/hex-twist-star-cfw-106) · [CFW 91](https://origami.kosmulski.org/instructions/hex-twist-star-cfw-91) · [CFW 143](https://origami.kosmulski.org/instructions/hexagonal-star-cfw-143) · [OrigamiUSA alternative](https://origamiusa.org/diagrams/six-pointed-star)
